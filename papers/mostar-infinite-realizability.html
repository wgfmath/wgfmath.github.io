Abstract

A nonnegative integer is infinitely realizable by the Mostar index if infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic connected graphs have that index. Alizadeh et al. proved that every nonnegative even integer is infinitely realizable and asked which odd integers have this property. We prove that every odd integer greater than one is infinitely realizable. Together with their results, this gives a complete characterization: a nonnegative integer is infinitely realizable if and only if it is different from one. Our constructions replace the vertices of an odd cycle by complete graphs, except at one or two positions occupied by independent pairs. A single two-parameter family realizes every odd integer at least five, and a second family realizes three. In particular, five is infinitely realizable by chemical graphs. We also prove that cycle expansions using only complete graphs cannot realize three or five, which explains the role of the independent pairs. Finally, we give a transmission formula for the parity of the Mostar index and derive consequences for Eulerian graphs, regular graphs, and uniformly random labelled graphs.

Keywords

Mostar index; inverse problem; infinite realizability; graph distance; parity.
